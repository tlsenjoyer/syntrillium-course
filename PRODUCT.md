# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Implementation choice delegated by issue #1: a local HTML/CSS/JavaScript sample
with bundled narration and SVG assets. It must open directly from disk after
download, without a server, build tool, account or speech service at playback.
Final full-course packaging remains open pending sample validation.

## Users

Russian-speaking learners studying introductory audio on Windows and macOS.
The course owner evaluates the representative lesson before full conversion.

## Product Purpose

Preserve Syntrillium's complete curriculum, lesson order, explanations, examples
and visualization meaning, with Russian visible text and spoken narration.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Visualizations are noninteractive. Playback controls may pause and seek the
lesson. Narration controls animation timing; Russian speech must not be cut off.
All playback resources must be bundled for fresh offline launches. No separate
example audio, added exercises, accounts or analytics are required.

## Brand Commitments

The original course is the visual reference, including its scientific diagram
language. Typography, layout and timing may change for Russian localization.
This is an adaptation, not a new visual identity or a curriculum rewrite.

## Evidence on Hand

`course.exe`, the recovery inventory and static composition, exported original
Flash vector paths, decoded English audio, and inspected Windows screenshots.
Automatic transcripts and the Russian sample remain review drafts. Original
continuous audiovisual comparison and macOS acceptance are outstanding.

## Product Principles

- Preserve educational content and examples; flag suspected errors for the owner.
- Correct confirmed scientific errors only with documented source wording,
  evidence, and explicit owner approval before narration generation.
- Keep production tools separate from files used by the learner.
- Make narration and visual event ordering explicit.
- Treat platform checks and human review as acceptance evidence, not assumptions.
